What is a metadata cleaner?

A metadata cleaner is a tool that removes the hidden information stored inside a file. Every photo you take and every image you generate carries more than just pixels. Cameras and phones write details such as the device model, lens settings, capture time and often the exact GPS location. Editing apps add their own names and version numbers. AI image generators can embed the prompt, model, seed and settings used to create the image, and some add C2PA Content Credentials that record where the image came from.

You usually can’t see any of this when you look at an image, but anyone who downloads the original file can read it with free tools. A single shared photo can reveal where you live, which phone you own or the full prompt behind a piece of AI art you spent hours refining.

AI Metadata Eraser is a free online metadata remover built for exactly this. It works as an EXIF remover, a GPS remover and an AI metadata remover in one step. Drop in your JPEG or PNG files and you get clean copies back, ready to share. Everything happens in your browser, so your images are never uploaded to a server.

What gets removed

Each cleaned image is rebuilt from its pixels alone, so none of the embedded data from the original file is carried over. That includes:

EXIF camera data

Camera or phone make and model, lens, focal length, exposure, ISO, flash and orientation settings.

GPS location

Latitude, longitude and altitude recorded when a photo was taken, which can point to your home or workplace.

Dates and times

The original capture date and time, plus digitized and modified timestamps written by cameras and editors.

Device identifiers

Camera body and lens serial numbers, and unique image IDs that can link different photos to the same device.

XMP and IPTC fields

Editing history, software names, author, copyright, captions and keywords added by apps like Photoshop and Lightroom.

C2PA Content Credentials

Embedded provenance manifests that record which tool or AI model created or edited an image.

AI generation parameters

Prompts, negative prompts, model names, seeds, samplers and settings stored in PNG text chunks by Stable Diffusion tools such as Automatic1111 and ComfyUI.

Thumbnails and extras

Embedded preview thumbnails, which can still show the uncropped original, plus maker notes and other private data blocks.

What stays the same

Your image itself. The cleaned copy keeps the same width, height and visible content. PNG files stay lossless. JPEG files are saved at high quality (96%), which looks the same to the eye in normal use. Embedded color profiles may be dropped, so images with wide-gamut color can look slightly different.

What a metadata cleaner can’t remove

Metadata removal only affects data stored alongside the pixels. It does not remove visible watermarks or logos, or invisible watermarks woven into the pixels themselves, such as Google SynthID. It can’t change records that a platform keeps on its own servers, and it doesn’t decide how an AI detector will classify an image. See the disclaimer for details.

How it works

  1. Add your images. Drag and drop JPEG or PNG files onto the cleaner, choose them from your device, or paste an image from your clipboard.
  2. Name your files (optional). Each file gets a random name by default, so the original filename doesn’t give anything away. You can type your own name or add a prefix to a whole batch.
  3. Process and download. Select Process and download. Each image is cleaned in your browser and saved to your device as a new file.
  4. Share with confidence. Post the clean copy wherever you like. Your original files stay untouched on your device.

Behind the scenes, the cleaner opens each image in your browser, draws only its pixels onto a blank canvas and exports that canvas as a brand-new JPEG or PNG file. Because the new file is created from scratch, metadata from the original has nowhere to go. This approach removes all embedded metadata at once instead of trying to find and delete individual tags, so unusual or hidden fields are cleaned too.

Why remove metadata before sharing?

Protect your location

Phone photos often include GPS coordinates. Removing them before you post, sell an item or send a picture to a stranger keeps your home, school or workplace private.

Keep your AI prompts private

Stable Diffusion and similar tools can store your full prompt and settings inside the image. Clean the file before sharing so your workflow stays yours.

Send cleaner client files

Designers, photographers and agencies can deliver images without internal filenames, software versions, author fields or editing history.

Sell safely online

Product photos for marketplaces like eBay, Etsy, Vinted or Facebook Marketplace don’t need to reveal where they were taken or which phone you use.

Some platforms remove part of the metadata when you upload, but rules differ between apps, change over time and often don’t apply to direct messages, file transfers, email or cloud links. Cleaning images yourself before you share them is the only way to know exactly what leaves your device.

No. File cleanup happens in your browser and downloads are created on your device. Our server handles sign-in and usage limits, but does not receive your files.

What metadata can it remove?

JPEG and PNG images are redrawn as new files. This removes embedded data such as EXIF camera details, GPS tags and PNG text fields, including some AI generation metadata. It does not remove visible or invisible watermarks, or guarantee how an AI detector will classify an image.

Will my images change?

JPEG images are re-encoded at high quality. PNG output is lossless, but browser rendering can change color profiles or other image properties. Your original files stay untouched. Each output downloads separately; your browser may ask you to allow multiple downloads.

How do I remove AI metadata from Midjourney, DALL·E or Stable Diffusion images?

Drop the JPEG or PNG file into the cleaner and select Process and download. The image is redrawn as a new file from its pixels only, so embedded AI generation data is left behind. This includes Stable Diffusion prompt and parameter text in PNG files, XMP tags and C2PA manifests.

Does it remove C2PA Content Credentials?

Yes, for C2PA data embedded in a JPEG or PNG file. The cleaned copy contains no C2PA manifest. It cannot remove invisible watermarks stored in the pixels themselves, such as Google SynthID, and cannot change records a platform keeps on its own servers.

Does it remove GPS location from photos?

Yes. GPS coordinates are stored in EXIF metadata, which is not carried over to the cleaned image. Camera make and model, serial numbers, capture dates and editing software tags are removed the same way.

Which file types are supported?

Metadata cleanup works with JPEG and PNG images. Other files, such as PDFs, videos, WebP or HEIC images, can be renamed in bulk, but their contents and metadata stay unchanged.
